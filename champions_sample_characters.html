Sample Characters

104 ready-to-play characters: 80 heroes and 24 villains, solo and in 16 teams, from 150-point agents to 750-point powerhouses.

Using These Characters

Every character on this page is an original, built with the rules from Character Creation. Most are standard superheroes on the Champions Complete default of 400 Total Points, of which 75 come from Complications (325 + 75). Use them as pre-made heroes for a one-shot, as allies and rivals for the GM, or as worked examples to pull apart when you build your own. None of them is a published Marvel, DC or Champions Universe character.

Power Levels

Character Creation leaves the total to the GM: street-level games sit below 400 points with a lower Complications cap, and heroes go above 400 when the GM sets a higher total or by spending Experience Points. This page uses these budgets:

  • 350, street level: one GM's choice of 300 base points plus up to 50 points of Complications. Another GM might pick a different number.
  • 400, standard: 325 base points plus up to 75 points of Complications.
  • 500, 600 and 750, veterans: experienced heroes on the standard 325 + 75, plus 100, 200 or 350 Experience Points, which buy things at the same prices as starting points.
  • Villains use the same budgets as heroes, from 400-point crooks to 750-point masterminds. Minions are built on 150 base points with no Complications, a budget one GM might set for henchmen.

The Points line on every card shows where its total comes from. Badges are purple for 350 and 400 points, orange for 500 points and up, and red for villains.

To use one, pick a hero whose concept appeals to you, rename them freely (names and day jobs are only suggestions), then adjust to taste. Move points between characteristics, powers and skills as you like, but keep the books balanced: characteristics + powers + skills must still come to the base points plus Complications plus any Experience Points.

Load them into your sheets. All 104 characters on this page can go straight into the Character Sheets: press Load sample characters there, or download the sample sheet file and use Import. Each sheet has the concept and identity, base points, Complications cap and any Experience (so the sheet's Total Points match the card), all seventeen characteristics, STUN, BODY and END ready to track, Resistant defenses, Flight under Movement, every power, skill and Complication, the card's description as Background and each power's description under Powers & Tactics. Villains and agents are marked as villains, and team members get the team's premise and full roster under Campaign Use.

Each card shows:

  • The character's concept and secret (or not so secret) identity under the name, and a round badge with their Total Points.
  • All seventeen characteristics: the main six, then the combat values and SPD, then defenses, REC, END, BODY and STUN. Each costs (value − base) × its cost per point, exactly as on the character sheet.
  • The main six rolls (9 + CHAR ÷ 5; INT's is the Perception Roll), the Speed Chart Segments the character acts on, and total defenses, with the Resistant part from powers in brackets.
  • Powers with Active Points, the Real Points actually paid and the END each use costs, built with the prices and modifiers on the Powers page.
  • Skills at 3 points for a full 9 + (CHAR ÷ 5) roll or 1 point for an 8- Familiarity, as on Skills, Perks & Equipment, and the character's Complications.

What these builds leave out. This guide prices a core set of powers (Blast, Killing Attack, Resistant Protection, Flight, Telepathy and Mind Control) and skills, so every character here is built from those. Martial Arts, Combat Skill Levels, extra Running, Teleportation, Hand-to-Hand Attack, Area of Effect, Perks and Talents all have their own prices in Champions Complete; add them from the core rulebook if you like, paying for them by trimming something else. Complication values follow the worked example in Character Creation and are a starting point: settle the exact value of each one with your GM from the Complication rules.

Rounding. Active Points, Real Points and END are rounded to the nearest whole number, with halves rounded in the character's favor, as in the Powers examples.
